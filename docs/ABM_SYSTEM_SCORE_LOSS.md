# ABM validation via the score of the auxiliary model

## Overview

A second and a third loss matrix for the same Model Confidence Set the project
already runs over impulse-response distance, built without fitting anything to
the simulated data.

`docs/ABM_SYSTEM_MCS_VALIDATION.md` describes the procedure this sits beside:
fit the driftless t-QVARMA(1,1,2) to the real US data and to every one of the
million simulated replicates, compute each fit's impulse response function, and
let the confidence set decide which of the 1000 ABM configurations cannot be
told apart from the closest one. That route needs a million fits, which is what
`out/abm_system_fit_qvarma/` holds and what took the bulk of the project's
compute.

The route documented here needs none of them. The real data's fitted parameter
vector is held fixed and each simulated series is asked a different question:
how hard would this series pull the estimate away from where the real data put
it. That is the score of the auxiliary model's log-likelihood, evaluated at the
real data's estimate on simulated data.

Everything here is produced by `applications/abm_system_score_loss.c` and
scored by `applications/abm_system_mcs_statistic_comparison.c`. Neither reads
`out/abm_system_fit_qvarma/`.

## The quantity

Write `theta_US` for the t-QVARMA parameter vector fitted to the US data,
stored in `out/us_qvarma_spec_choice_p1q1r2_fit.json` by
`applications/us_qvarma_spec_choice.c`. For configuration `i` and Monte Carlo
replicate `n`, with `y_in` that replicate's simulated five-variable series,

    q_in = d log L(theta_US ; y_in) / d theta

is the score: the gradient of the auxiliary model's log-likelihood with respect
to its parameters, evaluated at the real data's estimate but on simulated data.

It is zero exactly when the simulated series leaves `theta_US` at its own
maximum, and it grows with the direction and the size of the push the
simulation would give the estimate. A configuration whose dynamics resemble the
US data's scores low for the same reason its fitted impulse responses would sit
close, one estimation step earlier.

`theta` throughout is the unconstrained vector the optimizer steps, 42
coordinates for this specification, not the paper's own parameters. The score
is et_al.'s analytic gradient (`qvarma_analytic_log_likelihood` with a non-NULL
gradient argument), derived by hand from the filter recursion rather than
differenced, so it is exact to machine precision.

## Two losses, and why the second one exists

### The plain sum of squares

    out/abm_system_score_loss.csv      loss = q_in' q_in

This is the object as first specified. It has one defect, and it is not a small
one: it is not a distance in any metric the model supplies. The coordinates of
`theta` carry different units and differ in scale by orders of magnitude - an
intercept, an `atanh` of an autoregressive coefficient, a log of a scale
parameter, a `log(nu - 2)` - and a plain sum of squares weights whichever of
them happens to be largest. It also changes if a coordinate is rescaled, so
two parameterizations of the same model give two different rankings of the same
configurations.

Measured rather than argued, on `cop_0001` replicate 0, total 3.5684e+07:

| coordinate | share | cumulative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Omega_inv[2,0]` | 0.616 | 0.616 |
| `alpha2[0,0]` | 0.102 | 0.718 |
| `alpha1[0,0]` | 0.082 | 0.800 |
| `Omega_inv[2,2]` | 0.070 | 0.870 |
| `Omega_inv[2,1]` | 0.067 | 0.937 |

A single coordinate carries 62 per cent, and four entries of the scale matrix
carry three quarters between them. Row 2 is the employment series and row 0 is
GDP growth, so under this loss the confidence set is mostly ranking
configurations on one covariance. The manifest recomputes this table on every
run, beside the weighted split below.

It is kept, and it is kept first, because it is what the weighted version has
to be compared against.

### The score statistic

    out/abm_system_score_loss_weighted.csv
    loss = LM_in = q_in' [T_sim * I_1(theta_US)]^-1 q_in

with `I_1` the information matrix per observation and `T_sim` the length of a
simulated replicate. This is Rao's score statistic, equivalently the Lagrange
multiplier statistic, for the hypothesis that `y_in` was generated by the
auxiliary model at `theta_US`. Two properties make it the statistically
meaningful version of the same idea.

**It does not depend on how the model is parameterized.** Under a smooth
reparameterization `theta -> g(theta)` the score and the information transform
by the same Jacobian, `q -> J' q` and `I -> J' I J`, and the quadratic form
`q' I^-1 q` is unchanged. The weighted loss therefore measures the same thing
whether it is computed in the optimizer's unconstrained coordinates or in the
paper's own parameters, and the complaint against `q'q` above does not apply to
it. This is the whole reason for the weighting; the null distribution below is
a bonus.

**It has a reference distribution.** Under the hypothesis that a replicate came
from the auxiliary model at `theta_US`, `LM_in` is asymptotically chi-square on
42 degrees of freedom: mean 42, standard deviation 9.17. The numbers can be
read against that rather than only against each other.

## What the information matrix is, and where it comes from

`I_1` is estimated once, from the real data, as the observed information at the
estimate:

    I_US = -d2 log L(theta_US ; y_US) / d theta d theta'

over the 187 quarters the US block holds, divided by 187 to put it per
observation and multiplied by 400 to match a simulated replicate's length.
et_al.'s `_qvarma_hessian` differences the analytic gradient of the *negative*
log-likelihood, so what it returns is `I_US` directly, with no sign to get
wrong.

**One weighting matrix for every cell, from the real data.** The alternative -
estimating the variance of `q_in` separately within each configuration, from
its own thousand replicates - is tempting and wrong for this purpose. It gives
every configuration its own metric, and losses measured in different metrics
cannot be compared. Comparing them is the entire job of the confidence set.

**The price of that choice.** The chi-square calibration holds under the null
only. Where a configuration is far from the US data, the variance of `q_in`
under that configuration's own data-generating process is not `I_US`, so
`LM_in` is a distance in a fixed, model-supplied metric rather than a statistic
with a valid per-cell p-value. That is what the confidence set needs and it is
not more than that. The numbers below show the null is rejected in every cell
by a very large margin, so nothing in this project treats `LM_in` as a test.

**Conditioning.** The weighting divides by the eigenvalues of `I_US`, so its
conditioning is the weighting's own reliability, and the smallest eigenvalue of
a finite-differenced Hessian is the part of it known least accurately. The
manifest reports the spectrum, whether the matrix is positive definite, the
condition number, and how the statistic splits across eigen-directions on one
replicate, so that a weighting concentrated on a single direction would be
visible rather than assumed away.

No inverse is formed. The matrix `T_sim/T_US * I_US` is Cholesky-factored once,
before the parallel loop, and each cell costs one triangular solve: with the
factorization `L L'`, `LM = ||L^-1 q||^2`.

## The pipeline step

    make app-abm_system_score_loss              writes both matrices
    make app-abm_system_mcs_statistic_comparison   scores all three

Inputs:

- `dataset/abm_system/`, the simulated replicates.
- `out/us_qvarma_spec_choice_p1q1r2_fit.json`, the real-data estimate.
- `out/us_system.csv`, the real data itself, needed for the information matrix.

Outputs:

| file | what it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `out/abm_system_score_loss.csv` | `q'q`, 1000 replicates by 1000 configurations |
| `out/abm_system_score_loss_weighted.csv` | `LM`, same shape |
| `out/abm_system_score_loss_manifest.txt` | the weighting's diagnostics and every missing cell |

Both matrices carry the column names `applications/abm_system_irf_loss.c` uses
for the same configurations, so the three can be read side by side. A replicate
with a missing cell anywhere is dropped from every column of both, the
direction `abm_system_irf_loss.c` already drops in, because a hole in one
model's column loses every comparison that model takes part in and the
confidence set's p-values give no sign of it.

The archives are read one batch at a time through `abm_system_read_batch`,
added to `applications/abm_system.h` for this script. Reading a single
replicate decompresses the whole ten-replicate archive, so the per-replicate
route would decompress the dataset's 15 GB ten times over.

## Results

### Setup

1000 ABM configurations, 1000 Monte Carlo replicates each, five series per
replicate, 400 periods per replicate after the 200-period burn-in. Auxiliary
specification p1q1r2: `K = 5`, `K_star = 3`, `R = 1`, shared beta,
stationary-only `mu_star`, 42 unconstrained coordinates. `theta_US` read from
`out/us_qvarma_spec_choice_p1q1r2_fit.json`, which converged with gradient norm
0.00471282 over 1023 iterations. Information matrix differenced with step
1e-4 over the 187 real-data quarters. Every number below is float64. One
million cells per matrix, none missing in either.

Model Confidence Set settings, identical across all three matrices: alpha =
0.05, 10000 bootstrap resamples, block length 1 (the iid case, since replicates
are independent draws and not a time series), bootstrap variance of the
resampled mean, seed 123, stream 0. Both MCS statistics are scored on the same
resamples.

### The score at the real data's own estimate

`q'q` evaluated at `theta_US` on the US data itself is 2.221e-05, which is
0.00471282 squared, the gradient norm the fit reported. The vector being
plugged in is the one the fit produced, and the loss is essentially zero at the
data that produced it.

On simulated data the same quantity is around 3.5e+07: twelve orders of
magnitude larger.

### The weighting is not concentrated on one direction

The information matrix is positive definite: smallest eigenvalue 1.166e-01,
largest 2.239e+05, condition number 1.921e+06, no flat directions against a
floor of 2.088e-09.

A condition number of about two million is worth checking rather than waving
at, because the weighting divides by the eigenvalues and a statistic that lived
entirely in the flattest direction would be as one-dimensional as `q'q` is,
only less legibly. Splitting the statistic as `sum_k (v_k . q)^2 / lambda_k` on
`cop_0001` replicate 0:

| direction | eigenvalue | share | cumulative |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 23 | 3.570e+02 | 0.204 | 0.204 |
| 39 | 6.051e+03 | 0.146 | 0.350 |
| 24 | 3.660e+02 | 0.117 | 0.467 |
| 7 | 2.584e+01 | 0.108 | 0.575 |
| 25 | 4.161e+02 | 0.087 | 0.662 |

The largest single direction carries 20 per cent, against 62 per cent for the
largest single coordinate of `q'q`, and it takes five directions to reach two
thirds. The dominant contributors sit in the middle of the spectrum, not at the
flat end: the smallest eigenvalue, 0.117, is not among them, so the weighting
is not being driven by the part of the Hessian the differencing resolves worst.
The manifest recomputes this table on every run.

### The confidence sets

| loss | MCS_TR set | MCS_TMAX set | stopped because a test was accepted | final p |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IRF distance | `cop_0191` | `cop_0191` | no | 0.0011 |
| `q'q` | `cop_0931` | `cop_0931` | no | 0.0001 |
| `LM` | `cop_0409`, `cop_0599` | `cop_0409`, `cop_0599` | yes, round 999 | 0.1026 |

**The weighted statistic is the only one of the three that produces a
confidence set rather than a leftover.** Under the IRF distance and under
`q'q`, the procedure eliminates 999 configurations without ever accepting an
equivalence test: the singleton that remains is where elimination ran out, not
a set the data supports. Under `LM` the procedure stops because a test is
accepted, at p = 0.1026, with two configurations standing. The next one out,
`cop_0767`, leaves at p = 0.0002 under MCS_TR, so the boundary is not a close
call.

### How the three losses rank the configurations

Spearman rank correlation between the per-configuration mean losses, over all
1000 configurations:

| pair | rho |
| --- | --- |
| `LM` vs `q'q` | 0.9575 |
| `LM` vs IRF | 0.4919 |
| `q'q` vs IRF | 0.4773 |

The two score losses largely agree with each other and both disagree with the
impulse-response distance about as much as they agree with it. Of the 50
lowest-loss configurations under the IRF distance and under `q'q`, 11 are the
same.

The winners are not each other's runners-up:

| configuration | IRF rank | `q'q` rank | `LM` rank |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `cop_0191` | 1 | 363 | 354 |
| `cop_0931` | 4 | 1 | 15 |
| `cop_0409` | 375 | 3 | 1 |
| `cop_0599` | 305 | 7 | 2 |

### When each winner leaves the procedures it does not win

Rank by mean loss says how far down the field a configuration sits. It does not
say when the confidence set actually discards it, which is the question that
matters when three losses pick three different winners. The table below is the
round each winning configuration was eliminated in under each of the three
procedures, out of 999 elimination rounds over 1000 configurations. A higher
round means it survived longer. `MCS_TR` throughout, the statistic
`applications/abm_system_mcs.c` reports, so that one rule is being compared
across three losses rather than two rules across three losses.

| configuration | IRF | `q'q` | `LM` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `cop_0191` | kept | 627 | 657 |
| `cop_0931` | 998 | kept | 986 |
| `cop_0409` | 865 | 998 | kept |
| `cop_0599` | 806 | 996 | kept, round 999 |

Every elimination in that table is at an MCS p-value below 0.0001. The single
exception is `cop_0599` under `LM`, which is the configuration the accepted
test keeps at p = 0.1026.

The disagreement is asymmetric, and that is the useful part.

The three score winners survive each other's procedures almost to the end.
`cop_0931` lasts to round 998 of 999 under the impulse-response distance and
986 under `LM`; `cop_0409` lasts to 998 under `q'q`, `cop_0599` to 996. Under
the impulse-response procedure `cop_0409` and `cop_0599` go earlier, at 865 and
806, but still inside the last fifth of the field.

The impulse-response winner is not a near miss under either score loss.
`cop_0191` is discarded in round 627 and round 657, in the middle of the field,
with nothing marginal about either. Whatever the impulse-response distance is
responding to in `cop_0191`, neither score loss sees it as close.

This does not say the score losses are right and the impulse-response distance
is wrong. It says the two score losses, which correlate at 0.96 with each
other, identify a region of the design that the impulse-response distance does
not rate highly, and that the impulse-response distance's own winner is not on
the edge of that region but well outside it.

### Spread and scale

| loss | min | median | max | max/min |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `q'q`, mean per configuration | 3.038e+07 | 3.728e+07 | 4.819e+07 | 1.586 |
| `LM`, mean per configuration | 9.496e+03 | 1.421e+04 | 2.538e+04 | 2.673 |

Weighting widens the spread between configurations by a factor of about 1.7,
which is consistent with it being the reason the confidence set can now
separate them.

Over all million cells of the weighted matrix the statistic runs from 5.823e+03
to 3.815e+04, median 1.421e+04. Against a chi-square reference with mean 42,
every cell rejects the null that the replicate came from the auxiliary model at
`theta_US`, by a factor of roughly 340 at the median. That is expected and it
is not a defect: the ABM is not a t-QVARMA, and no configuration of it was ever
going to be. It does mean the statistic functions here as a ranking distance
and not as a test, which is what the section above says.

### The two MCS statistics agree far better under the score losses

Spearman rank correlation between the MCS_TR and MCS_TMAX elimination orders:

| loss | rho | median round gap | largest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IRF distance | 0.3758 | 246 | 767 |
| `q'q` | 0.9992 | 6 | 46 |
| `LM` | 0.9981 | 6 | 101 |

Under the impulse-response distance the choice of MCS statistic reorders the
field almost arbitrarily; under either score loss it barely matters. Both sets
in the table above are the same under TR and TMAX.

## What this does not establish

The procedure ranks configurations against one another. It does not test
whether the best one is close to the US data in any absolute sense, and the
chi-square numbers above say plainly that it is not: every replicate of every
configuration is rejected as a draw from the auxiliary model at the real data's
estimate. `docs/ABM_SYSTEM_MCS_VALIDATION.md`, "What the confidence set does
not say", makes the same point for the impulse-response route and its
measurements apply here unchanged.

The three losses disagree about which configuration is closest. Nothing in this
document establishes which of them should be believed. What it does establish
is that the choice matters, and by how much: the impulse-response winner is
eliminated in round 657 of 999 by the weighted score loss, which is mid-field
and not a close call, while the weighted score loss's own winners survive the
impulse-response procedure into its last fifth. The disagreement is one-sided
in that sense, and the section "When each winner leaves the procedures it does
not win" has the rounds.

The information matrix is a finite-difference Hessian, not an analytic one. Its
smallest eigenvalue, which the weighting divides by and therefore amplifies
most, is the quantity in this whole calculation that the differencing step
resolves least well. An analytic second derivative would remove that doubt and
et_al. does not have one.

## Where the general primitives belong

`_qvarma_hessian` now has a second caller. Its own comment in
`et_al./sd/qvarma.h` says it lives in the model file only until that happens: a
numerical second derivative of a scalar objective is general and belongs in a
lower layer. It should move into et_al.'s solver or statistics module, and this
project's use of it is the case that qualifies it.
