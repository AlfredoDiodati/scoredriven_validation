Whether the simulated interest rate has a GARCH effect.

Test: McLeod-Li, the Ljung-Box test on the squared residuals of an
autoregression of the rate in levels with an intercept and 4 lags, at 4
and 8 lags of the squares. Rejection is p < 0.05.

Columns. 'samples' is how many series were tested. 'degenerate' is how
many could not be, because the squared residuals were constant over a
window the test correlates, as they are for a run on the floor
throughout. 'reject L' is the share of the others rejected at L lags.
'acf1 sq' is the mean first-order autocorrelation of the squared
residuals.

PART 1. Calibration. 10000 samples of 400 values from each law, seed 20261016,
x_t = (1 - phi) mu + phi x_(t-1) + e_t with phi = 0.9. The Gaussian, the
Student t4 and the floored AR(1) have no GARCH effect; the GARCH(1,1) has
omega = 0.05, alpha = 0.10, beta = 0.85.

  law                          samples degenerate   reject 4   reject 8      acf1 sq
  Gaussian                       10000          0     0.0465     0.0542      -0.0051
  Student t4                     10000          0     0.0467     0.0614      -0.0045
  GARCH(1,1)                     10000          0     0.7890     0.8396       0.1216
  floored AR(1)                  10000          0     0.0656     0.0745       0.0008

PART 2. The experiment's interest rate, 1000 configurations, 1000000
replicates, the 400 quarters after burn-in of each, one test per replicate.
A quarter is on the floor when the stored rate is at most 1.5e-04 per cent.
0.6 minutes.
